### Introduction

Speaking well is a soft skills that needs to practice. When you speak well, you gain greater confidence in work and profession. It also enhances your career prospects through networking. You will enjoy better relationships in work and personal life. Speaking well goes together with sharper writing conversational skills.

In AI era, speak well is one of the main factors that separate human from the AI. It enhances greater employability you are able to communicate in human way with heart and warm.

> Written words reflect your thought process. Speaking words says who you are.

### Be Articulate

Articulate is being able to express a thought or idea fluent

1. Be Fluent: Fluency is an ability to speak with natural and easy flow, without struggling to find the right words and right grammatical structures.

**Action**
- pick a sentence
- re-write it in 3 or more different ways to express

2. Avoid vague words.
"Thank you for the nice dinner."
"That is a good suggestion." instead, use "timely/useful/helpful suggestion"

3. Avoid filler words.

It is okay to use, not too much.
eg. you know, you get what I mean?, understand?, I guess, I think, like, um, er
**Action:**
Use silence instead.

4. Use nuancing.

Origianl: John lacks empathy.
With Nuancing:
- John is somewhat lacking in empathy.
- John seems to lack empathy.
- John comes across as lacking empathy.

5. Pronounce every word fully.

Pronounce a word fully and distinctly. Especially "th", "t" and "k".
Example: I think I distinctly heard that a long way back.

### Be Expressive

Being able to communicate effectively using verbal expressions to conjure and image

1. Speak with Vocal Variety (Tonality)

Trying reading the lyric. Your words come alive like in a song.
Elements of Vocal Variety - Tone, Pitch, Pace and Pause

(A) Tone: Emotional Character of your voice
(B) Pitch: Low Pitch - serious, calm, Authoritative voice; High Pitch: excited or anxious voice

> Don't end a statement like a question.

(C) Pace - speak at a moderate speed.
(D) Pause - Before making a point of emphasis, Pause. Then pause again.

> Speak to convey, Pause to connect

2. End a sentence for effect

Head [Context] > Core > Tail [Matter to be stressed]
Putting the most important word puts at the end.

Original:
John's performance has been outstanding in the last three years.
Better:
In the last 3 years, John's performance has been "outstanding".

Standard:
I will not exploit my opponent's youth and inexperience for political purposes.
Reagan's construction:
I will not exploit, for political purposes, my opponent's youth and experience.

"That's what you've set yourself out to conquer.
And conquer it you will."
- Prof Henry Higgins in "My Fair Lady"

3. Use Expressive Prose

Use idioms, metaphors and expressive words.
Be mindful if the listener is able to understand.

### Be engaging

Being able to inspire and hold attention

1. Use humour

Forms of Humour: 
- a humorous story
- a witty remark
- a tongue-in-cheek remark

eg. "Buy 10 eggs. Free 2" - "which two eggs got free"

How did you get out from bad joke?

2. Make eye contact

- Eye contact connects you with listeners, especially during a pause.
- Locked eyes raise oxytocin level.
- When pausing to think, avoid gazing up.

> Eyes are pathways to the heart.

3. Engage the Listeners

- Ask questions.
- Involve audience in some activities.
- Walk towards or among the audience.

4. Use body language

- Hand gestures
- Eye contact
- Body movements

5. Tell Stories

- Helps listeners understand an idea or concept
- A story triggers Dopamine and Oxytocin
- Stories are the most memorable parts of any speech (anywhere, beginning, middle and ending)

**Activity**

- Approaching the Rostrum (walk to rostrum, smile as you approach near the rostrum)
- Show respect gesture
- Scan the room first

### Speaking Situations

Conversations

- Kopi chat
- Dialogue/ Fireside chat
- Corporate Networking
- Interview/ Q&A

**Conversation**

- Be open minded
- Show mutual respect
- Be concise; avoid rambling
- Avoid giving extreme views
- Don't talk too much about yourself; ask about the other person
- Don't hog the conversation

**Corporate Networking**

- Check who will be at the event
- Go prepared with current news
- Use non-standard greetings (eg. how are you? - what are your latest news?)
- Use interesting self-introductions (not by job title, use fun fact)
- Ask open-ended questions
- Avoid "talking shop"

Sharon: "I am the Director of Systems Development at Axel System Solutions."
Sharif: "I am the Director of Systems Development at Meta Business Ideas. I am also a guitarist."
Suresh: "I am a guitarist". When I am not playing my guitar, I am the Director of Systems Development at Business Transformation."

Suresh probably be picked as he appeared as open, funny and mentally more energized.

**Talking to "Stranger"**

1. Use small talk.
2. Open you up to New ideas and Opportunities.
3. Increase your feelings and happiness, sense of connection and self-confidence.

> Interview like a Dialogue, not an Interrogation

#### Interview Technique

1. Asking a question

- ask an open-ended question
- set the context to the question

2. Answering question

- answer in conversational style
- tell a story

3. Asking next question

- react to that answer that you just heard
- then ask your question (with a context)

> Answering a loaded, or difficult, question
Using deflection

**Work Meeting**

- Prepare your comments in advance
- At appropriate moment, make your point.
- Your intro: Connect with the last point made.
- Do not be afraid of being the "lone voice".
- (The meeting chairman) Be the last to speak.

**Leading & Supervising**

> You must have interest in people.

- Overall tone in a performance review meeting should be conversational, thereby creating an atmosphere of transparency and trust.
- Give feedback in a positive and constructive way.
- Be specific in your judgement.
- Speak less and listen more.

**Public Speaking**

- manuscript speaking (reading script)
- extemporaneous speaking (outline)
- impromptu speaking (without outline)

Tip 1: Opening of a Speech
It must be a strong opening

Tip 2: Short Sentences, Familiar words
SLIM index

Tip 3: Present points as connection
Firstly,
Secondly,
Thirdly,

Tip 4: Connect sentences for flow

Tip 5: "Lead in" each new idea

Tip 6: Closing speech.

> I am sorry to have given you such a long speech. If I had more time, I would have a shorter speech.
> Winton Churchill

**Practice**: Do it often.

**Guide for Impromptu speeches**:

Body - Your elaboration
Closing - Summarize your points
